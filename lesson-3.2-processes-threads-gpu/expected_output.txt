Part 3: why this number matters for what comes next
  every one of those 1000 threads above involved the operating system:
  a stack allocation, a scheduler registration, real kernel-level
  bookkeeping. that's why it costs microseconds per thread, not
  nanoseconds -- spawning a million OS threads this way would take
  seconds, and nobody does that on a CPU for good reason.
  a GPU kernel launched in Module 5 will routinely start a million
  threads in one call. that only works because a GPU thread is not
  an OS thread -- no separate stack allocation, no scheduler
  negotiation, no kernel-level bookkeeping per thread. what a GPU
  thread actually is gets built in Module 5, on top of this contrast.
